The moment
Tuesday, 2:40pm — oncology clinic

A patient just tested positive for an EGFR exon 19 deletion. Four FDA-approved drugs target it. The physician has treated maybe a dozen cases like this — not enough to have felt the real-world difference between them. Each drug's trial data lives in a different paper. None of them show her how the drugs actually bind this patient's specific mutation, side by side.

She has to choose today. The tools available to help her haven't changed in a decade.

OncoBind Copyright © 2026 OncoBind
The problem, zoomed out

This isn't one physician's problem. It's structural.

  • Molecular subtyping now routinely identifies the exact mutated target — but for most such targets there are now three to five FDA-approved drugs, not one, and the count keeps growing.
  • Selection is guided largely by approval recency and clinician familiarity, not comparative structural or binding data, which is scattered across separate primary-literature sources for each drug.
  • Adverse-effect tradeoffs — a black-box cardiovascular warning versus a milder but less potent alternative — are buried in separate, non-comparative prescribing documents, not surfaced at the moment of decision.
5 major kinase-target families. 17+ approved drugs across them. No structure-based comparison tool built for the point of care — until now.

The solution

OncoBind turns that decision into a structural comparison, live.

For a diagnosed molecular subtype, OncoBind docks every approved candidate drug against the real target structure, predicts binding affinity, and combines it — transparently — with adverse-effect burden and the individual patient's context into one explainable score.

Dock

DiffDock predicts the real 3D binding pose of each candidate against the real receptor structure.

→

Predict

Boltz2 predicts binding affinity for the same ligand-target pair — same model class that redefined structural biology.

→

Score

A transparent, published formula combines affinity, docking confidence, and adverse-effect burden.

→

Personalize

Patient-specific factors re-weight the score live — the same drug ranks differently for different patients.

Why trust it

No black box. Every number traces to a named model or a public database.

Unlike opaque "AI risk score" tools, OncoBind's scoring is a published, auditable formula — the same one its in-app AI advisor is instructed to explain verbatim, on request, to any physician who asks.

// composite_score, normalized within each scenario's candidate drugs
composite_score = 100 × clamp(0.45 × affinity_norm + 0.35 × docking_norm + 0.20 × (1 − adverse_norm))
// patient_adjusted_score layers on top, per flagged clinical factor
patient_adjusted = clamp(composite_score − matched_risk_penalty + matched_relevance_bonus, 0, 100)
